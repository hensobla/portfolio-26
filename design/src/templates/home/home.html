01 Lead product designer · 2025

Ledger

A proof-of-ownership layer for physical goods — every product carries a living record from the factory floor to its third owner.

Role
Lead product designer
Team
2 designers · 4 engineers
Timeline
9 months
Surface
Web + iOS

Ledger started as a warranty tool and became the spine of the brand's resale program. I owned the end-to-end flow: claiming a product, transferring it, and reading its history as a single annotated timeline.

The hard part was making provenance feel tangible. We treated each record like an exploded diagram — every repair, every owner, every part swap labelled and placed in sequence, so trust came from legibility rather than a badge.

02 Product design lead · 2024

Crate

A checkout that ships inside the box — reorder, register, and resell a physical product from the packaging it arrived in.

Role
Product design lead
Team
1 designer · 3 engineers
Timeline
6 months
Surface
Web · QR entry

Crate collapses the post-purchase gap. A code on the box opens a pre-filled session that already knows the product, the buyer, and the warranty window — no account wall, no re-keying an address.

I designed the flow to degrade gracefully: a full configurator on desktop, a two-tap reorder on mobile, and a printed fallback card for when there's no signal on the warehouse floor.

03 Design + prototyping · 2024

Atlas

An exploded-diagram product viewer — spin a thing apart, read every part, and add any of them to cart without leaving the drawing.

Role
Design + prototyping
Team
2 designers · 2 engineers
Timeline
4 months
Surface
Web · WebGL

Atlas turns the spec sheet into the storefront. Each component is a labelled node in a 3D explosion; tapping a label pulls the part, surfaces its price and lead time, and lets you swap it in place.

I prototyped the interaction in the browser first — drag to orbit, scroll to explode — then specced the handoff so engineering could drive it from the same parts manifest the factory already used.

04 Staff product designer · 2023

Manifold

A configurator for made-to-order hardware — every option a real constraint, every constraint shown before you commit.

Role
Staff product designer
Team
1 designer · 5 engineers
Timeline
11 months
Surface
Web · internal + buyer

Manifold replaced a 40-field spreadsheet order form. I modelled the product as a graph of compatible parts so the UI could grey out impossible combinations live instead of failing at the loading dock.

The design language borrowed from engineering drawings: monospace part numbers, dimensioned previews, and a running bill of materials that updated as you built — the price was never a surprise.
