01

The brief, then the reframe

Most case studies open with what they were asked to do. The interesting part of this one is what I argued they should do instead.

What I was asked
Update the configurator UI to match the 2025 brand refresh. Same flow, new look.
What I argued for
The configurator wasn't a UI problem. It was a decision problem. We were forcing a variant choice before the buyer had earned the context for it.

The trigger was a 90-minute working session with PM and the eng lead, where I showed three different cohort cuts of the funnel data. In every cut, the drop-off cliff was at the variant grid, not at price, not at shipping, not at sign-in. The brand refresh was the surface deliverable. The underlying intervention had to be a restructure of the decision flow itself: explore use cases first, then narrow.

By the end of that session, PM had reframed the OKR mid-quarter from "configurator refresh" to "configurator conversion rate." Scope expanded. Timeline did too. The brand refresh shipped inside the new flow rather than dictating it.

02

Hypothesis & test design

Hypothesis

Buyers who explore use cases before variants will convert at higher rates because they will have constructed their own justification for the purchase by the time they reach the variant grid.

Mechanism: self-persuasion (Aronson) compounded with commitment-consistency (Cialdini). The use-case selector functions as a small, low-cost commitment that the variant grid then collects on.

04

What we tested. What won. What lost.

Three arms. V2 is the cautionary tale; it lifted CVR but tanked AOV. V3 won because it added a guided narrative that anchored on a higher-value use case before showing variants.

V1
Control
Baseline

Existing flow: buyer lands on a 14-tile variant grid, sorted by SKU. No context, no narrative, no use-case framing. The brand refresh was applied here cosmetically.

Result CVR baseline · AOV baseline
V2
Use-case selector
Lost

Use-case selector first, then a simplified variant grid. We thought the framing alone would do the work.

Result CVR +14%
AOV −9%
V3
Use case → narrative → grid
Winner

Use case → guided narrative paragraph (we wrote 8 of these, one per use case) → variant grid with the recommended variant pre-selected based on use case. Buyers could change it. Most didn't.

Result CVR +18%
AOV +6%

The lesson from V2: removing friction without adding context just makes the cheapest option easier to find. The lesson from V3: the guided narrative isn't decoration, it's the part of the experience that does the persuasion work. The variant grid is just where the transaction completes.

05

Roads we didn't take

Alt. A

Quiz format

Killed

Five-step quiz to recommend a variant. Killed because quiz fatigue is real and our session data showed cohorts who quizzed elsewhere on the site disengaged 12% faster on subsequent sessions. Asking buyers to "earn" a recommendation through interrogation is the wrong energy for a $1,500 purchase.

Alt. B

Live chat assist at variant step

Killed

A "talk to a specialist" CTA at the moment of variant indecision. Killed because CX headcount couldn't support real-time response, and an async fallback would make latency the problem rather than the solution. Bad CX worse than no CX here.

Alt. C

AI-generated personal recs

Tabled for v4

LLM reading session signals and generating a personalized variant recommendation paragraph. The personalization data infrastructure wasn't there yet, and rushing it would have meant generic recs that erode trust on a high-stakes purchase. v4, once the infra ships.

Outcomes at 3 months & 6 months

The interesting result wasn't the headline lift. It was what the use-case framing did to the surfaces it touched downstream.

    CVR · primaryTarget was +12. Held confidence interval throughout.
  • +6%
    AOV · secondaryNo target. The use-case framing anchored buyers on higher-value variants.
  • +47%
    Time on configuratorEngagement up because the narrative was readable, not friction.
  • −22%

+ 6 mo.

  • +4%

Second-order surprise

PDP pages connected to the configurator outflow saw +8% CVR independently, even though we hadn't touched them. Updating PDP copy to mirror the use-case framing carried the persuasion structure backward into the funnel. Merch is now applying the use-case framing across the catalog.

08 — What I'd do next
The use-case selector is the real product. The variant grid is just where the transaction completes.

v4 should rebuild the experience around use case as the navigation primitive across the entire site, with the variant grid demoted to a final-mile step. PDP, search, even category navigation should organize around use case before SKU. The 6-month data already suggests this is where the leverage is.

v5 should layer in the AI-generated personal recommendation paragraph that we tabled in V3 alternatives. The personalization data infrastructure is shipping in Q3. The work is to make that recommendation feel earned, not generated, with explicit citations to the buyer's session signals.

09

How I worked with AI on this

Workflow

Cursor for the front-end prototype of the use-case selector and the narrative-block component. Claude for the eight use-case narrative drafts, with a brand-voice reference doc and three rounds of human edit per draft. Figma Make for the early-stage layout exploration before the prototype branched into Cursor.

The AI never wrote final copy. It produced the first draft of each narrative against the use-case definition merch had authored, and I (and brand, and merch) edited toward our voice from there. The pattern that worked: AI does the cold start; human does the taste.

What AI couldn't do: the working session that produced the reframe. The conversations with CX about ticket patterns. The judgment call to kill V2 four days early when AOV started bleeding. None of those are prompt-shaped.

Counterfactual
5 weeks · 3 ppl ~3 wks · solo

Pre-AI estimate: 1 designer + 1 front-end engineer + 1 copywriter, ~5 weeks. Actual: solo, AI-amplified, ~3 weeks.

Compression came from prototyping in real code (eng didn't have to translate Figma) and from drafting all 8 narrative blocks in one afternoon instead of one per week.
